The Strainer

The Strainer is a infinite runner mobile game for android, it's similar to games like subway surfers and temple run but with a horror game twist and a ps1 style art direction!

As the PROGRAMMER for this assignement, I'm tasked with coding all gameplay features and putting all models, textures, and animations into the game engine and getting them assembled!

Below is my Development Process where I got over the ups and downs, struggles, and things I'm proud of with the development of the project.

Status: In Development

Development Updates & Devlog

Sprint #4

This sprint, because of how far along I've gotten the programming in the game, I decided that it was best to instead focus on working on some of the textures that we would need for the game, over the course of the sprint I finished 2 full textures sets, or about 25 models, and got 28 points done overall, managing to outdo many people this sprint, I also decided that the best way to get this done was to spam them all for a few days and do very little else, allowing me to get all 25 textures done in just a few days, which saved me time in the long run.

One probably I did have though were some of the UVs, a lot of people do their UVs with the intent that they would be the one doing textures, and my team is no different, but this does unfortunately cause some problems when someone ELSE ends up doing the vertex painting and texturing, leading to me having to make certain small changes to the UVs and Models, which caused even more work.

Texturing Issues:


I also ran into certain UVs problems that caused some Vertex Bleeding, I spent a lot of time trying to fix this, and even though I managed to fix most of it, some things, like one of the broken power lines, still had them and I couldn’t find a way to fix them, but I managed to find a way to work around this when texturing them, it even sort of adds to the style of the textures thanks to this which I like quite a bit.

One of the only programming things I did was to work on the Rendering Issues for the build on Mobile, these were issues like The Strainer model not appearing and the VHS effect on the post processing causing some problems with the camera when displayed on mobile, most of the issues were resolved after a few days of the combined work of Me and Andrew, however one problem was still really hard for us to solve, and that was the issue of the VHS effect messing up the camera, we tried everything we could to fix it, but at the end of the day, the only was we could find a way to fix it was gutting the effect, reducing it to about 1/5th of the size it originally was and cutting the majority of the features.

New VHS:



However, on some better news, pretty much all of the textures turned out well, and thanks to the texturing pipeline I designed previously, they turned out really well and looked great, perfectly fitting the style.

All in all, this sprint contained very little negatives for me, and it was a nice break and change of pace to not have to worry about cramming programming and getting to a point count that made me happy, this sprint helped me put the work I usually do into perspective and made me realized that I texture MUCH faster than I can program, she isn’t a bad thing, it just means that, in all, programming takes more effort for me, but that also results in better and more polished systems, which left be in a better headspace to jump back into finishing up the last bit of programming and incorporating playtest feedback in the next sprint!

At our current rate, we’ll have about a sprint and a half of polish time at the end, which is more than enough for us and gives us plenty of time to make sure this game is perfect and up to our standards!

Sprint #3

Our third sprint may be our most productive sprint yet, I managed to get 27 points done, I was hoping to get to 30 but had a little bit of trouble on a 3 point card at the end so I didn’t get around to it

Something I spent a lot of time on this sprint was getting animations set up, we’ve been getting a lot coming in from our Animator so I’ve had a lot to work with and experiment with. I had a bit of knowledge of Animation Graphs and State Machines, but I managed to learn quite a bit more this time around, I even managed to learn something I couldn’t figure out during my Lethal Company project, which was how to push animations to only certain parts of the body, like being able to play idle, attacking, holding, etc on the upper body while the lower body continues to move and run, which is very helpful for this project and future ones. I also taught myself about animation slots and montages, overall I learned a lot and have a newfound respect for riggers and especially people who name the bones in their rigs!

Something I did that I think came out really well has to be the skin menu. We realized that with our current scope, we had time to add in more features so we decided to make player skins, it was a fun system to get implemented, and working on the UI for it made me realise that even though I’m a programmer I could also definitely see myself as a UI Designer, it is pretty fun and I’ve loved how all the UI for this game turned out and I could definitely see myself doing it professionally.

Skins Menu:


Another thing that turned out really well was the quicktime event when being caught by The Strainer, when you’re caught, if you have level 1, 2, or 3 of the Escape upgrades, you have a chance to escape from the strainer 1, 2, or 3 times respectively. Normal, when you’re caught, you just die, but with these perks, you can do a spam click quicktime event to break away from him and continue your run, which serves to really make the player feel like they are stronger and that their upgrades actually have a real effect on their runs, especially since the LVL 1 Escape Upgrade is one of the first ones you can buy!

Strainer Quicktime Event:


The thing I have struggled with the most is my job as the build engineer. We've been running into near constant rendering issues ONLY on our mobile build that I cannot seem to solve, I’ve been spending countless hours attempting to fix these issues, but everything I do and follow seem to only make things worse. Our two main issues are The Strainer’s Skeletal Mesh not appearing and lighting, resulting in our post processing material either not displaying properly or not displaying at all. Both of the previously mentioned things are INCREDIBLY important parts of our game and something we can’t just deal with, but yet during this sprint I have not found a single way to fix it, in fact they’ve only seemed to get worse, I will be working on this moving forward but I’m not totally sure what to do.

Overall we’ve had a really good sprint, and all of the programming for the game SHOULD be finished this sprint, which’ll have me most likely doing some texturing & level design work moving forward after this, which I’ve already started to dabble into, all in all, our game is coming along great so far!

Sprint #2

February 22th 2026

For our second sprint, we’ve again managed to get a lot done. I somehow managed to get the exact same amount of points done as the first sprint, the only difference being this time one of them was a three point card. Our team overall got a little bit less done, however I believe the cause of that is that one of our group members started working on animations and rigging, which does take quite a lot of time compared to everything else we’re doing, so it makes sense.

Over the course of this sprint, I got quite a bit done, a lot of which I’ve very proud of; The Upgrade Tree, Implementation of Every Upgrade, Description Pages of every Upgrade, Speed Increased, Falling Light Poles, The beginnings of the Enemy, The Main Title Screen, Working Settings, The Introductory Cutscene, and the code behind the animation states for the Enemy! It definitely took a lot of work, and even though it was the same amount of points as Sprint 1, it felt like a lot more work, but I am also extremely proud of how everything turned out, especially all the UI Work I did

The three point card I did, which definitely took a lot of time, was the upgrade tree, and that isn’t even counting the implementation of all the upgrades, some of which are fairly complex, like the boost upgrade, which unlocks a boost for the player. The upgrade tree is a large, easily expandable tree that the player can drag around, and each upgrade can be held to pull up a description page of them, showing the name, description, and price!

Upgrade Tree:


Something I worked on that was very new to me was the settings menu, it sounds pretty easy, but setting up sliders for volume and brightness is something I’d never done before and had no idea to go about doing, lucky, the sliders built into the Unreal UI were very intuitive, however there still came the problem of actually attaching volumes changes to them. I ended up teaching myself about Sound Classes and Mixes, which when put together, allow to you apply changes to different categories of sounds across the game, which lucky were very easy to attach to the sliders, which I was able to test using free use music online, which I will be changing later when we get finalized music for the game.

Settings Page:


Something I’m very proud of that I got working was the Camera Movement system, I’ve done a few Camera Movement systems before but this is probably my most advanced one, taking placable points around the world and adding all their references to a map, added under keys that can be set in the world, then allowing the camera to interpolate between them using Timeline and Ease nodes, getting a very smooth and easy to program camera system that I had a lot of fun making, it took a decent bit to get it fully working, and it had a few bugs, but thankfully it works perfect now and I’m very proud of it! The following video shows off the Camera System in the intro cutscene, this’ll probably be changed slightly once I have all of the models and player animations, but it’s pretty great already!

Intro Cutscene:

Sprint #1

February 8th 2026

Our first sprint working on this project was roughly two weeks and boy did we get a lot done, my entire team got 36 cards down, and I did 20. During this sprint I got an entire working prototype down complete with a full UI, shader effects, movement, and road and background generation done, which picks from sets to randomize based on the area

Something I'm very proud of that took me quite a bit of time was the UI, I had the idea to maker the shader and UI resemble a VHS Camcorder to go along with the horror theme, and it took a very long time and a lot of editing, and to be honest I need to go back through and clean up the nodes a lot, but it turned out looking great and I'm very proud of it!'

UI and Shaders:


Something I unfortunately struggled a lot on was the placing of the backgrounds, which had an issue where even after you changed locations, it wouldn't start spawning the correct background sets for at least 100 KMs In Game, which was a big problem because it meant that players had to go way too far before seeing a transition to a new location which isn't good, Lucky I managed to fix it by turning down the distance internally.

Spawning Code:

Another big thing that I got done this sprint was working on all of the movement components and inputs, which definitely wasn't easy at first, not only had I absolutely never worked with mobile inputs for Unreal before, but it also wasn't like I could even test them on a phone yet, so I had to look into how to test phone inputs on computer, eventually finding some settings that used the mouse to simulate the inputs of a finger.

Despite me finding this setting, which definitely helped, Unreal's input system for mobile is still a bit primitive, which resulted in my having to run all of the 4 swipe inputs, Up, Down, Left, and Right, off of one input node, which was kind of challenging at first but when I eventually got it I was pretty happy, and since that point, setting up inputs for things has been surprisingly easy.'
